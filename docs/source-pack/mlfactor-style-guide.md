# Style Guide

## Voice

- Lead with the conclusion.
- Be finance-native, skeptical, and practical.
- Avoid academic tone and avoid hype.
- Keep the reader oriented toward decisions, not abstract ML trivia.

## Structure

- Use short cards and compact sections.
- Prefer comparison tables, callouts, and hover panels over long paragraphs.
- Explain each idea in the same pattern when possible:
  - What it is
  - Why an asset manager cares
  - Inputs
  - Outputs
  - Strengths
  - Weaknesses
  - Investor translation

## Framing rules

- Do not use fake precision.
- Prefer probabilistic language over point estimates.
- Highlight similarity, difference, implication, and falsification.
- Treat ML as a tool for structure and uncertainty, not a market oracle.
- Use examples that a portfolio manager or analyst would actually recognize.

## UX rules

- Put the thesis high on the page.
- Keep navigation sticky and obvious.
- Make expandable details available on click or hover.
- Keep the layout responsive and easy to scan on mobile.
- Avoid over-animated or gimmicky interactions.

## Content discipline

- Do not fabricate citations or data.
- Do not copy external text.
- Keep copy editable in source files.
- Use the source pack for background, not as the main content surface.
